## Set the policy before adding the button
A cancellation link is useful only when the customer understands what happens next. State the latest time they can cancel or move an appointment, whether payment is refundable, and how to contact you after the cutoff. Display the policy on the service page and repeat the relevant deadline in the confirmation.

Avoid a vague “24-hour policy” when customers may be in different time zones. For a Tuesday appointment at 14:00 in Paris, show the actual cutoff and time zone. Set different rules for a short consultation and a full-day private event if their preparation costs differ.

## Map the four possible outcomes
| Customer action | Availability outcome | Payment decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cancels within the allowed window | Release the occupied slot | Apply the published refund policy |
| Reschedules to another available slot | Release the old slot and reserve the new one | Keep, adjust or separately refund the payment |
| Requests a change after the cutoff | Keep the booking until staff decide | Review the applicable policy |
| Does not attend | Record the no-show accurately | Follow the disclosed policy and payment provider rules |

A canceled Shopify order and a canceled booking are not automatically the same event in every app. Confirm which event triggers the other in your setup. Never assume a successful booking cancellation proves a card refund happened.

## Build a customer journey people can follow
1. Put one clearly labeled **Manage booking** link in the confirmation.
2. Show the current service, date, time, location and time zone before a change.
3. Offer only genuinely available replacement slots.
4. Ask the customer to confirm the change once.
5. Send the final details and invalidate the old calendar invitation where supported.

If your app does not provide a particular self-service action on your plan, give customers a working support route. Do not display a button that sends them to a generic homepage.

## Check staff and calendar behavior
Create two test bookings with the same member of staff. Move the first into a time that would overlap the second, including cleanup time. The system should reject the conflict. Then move it into a valid slot and verify the staff calendar, customer email, booking dashboard and storefront agree.

Test external calendar sync separately. Deleting an event in Google Calendar may not cancel the paid Shopify booking. Document the authoritative place for your team to make changes rather than relying on whichever screen is closest.

## Keep an audit trail for exceptions
Record who approved an exception, what changed and whether a refund was issued. A short internal note is usually more useful than a long email chain. Track cancellations, reschedules and no-shows separately: they are different operational problems.

Review the reasons monthly. If customers frequently move bookings because arrival instructions are unclear, improve the confirmation. If your schedule changes often, fix staff availability before tightening customer penalties.
