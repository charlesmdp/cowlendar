## Define what one rental consumes
List the resources required by a booking: the item, accessories, staff time and any preparation or cleaning period. A kayak booking might consume a boat, paddle and guide. A camera rental may reserve a particular kit for several consecutive days. The reservation must protect the scarce resource for the whole interval.

Write down your inventory units before installing an app. Decide whether customers select a named item or any available unit within a category. That distinction affects how you handle substitutions, repairs and maintenance.

## Which apps belong on the shortlist?
Cowlendar, BookX and BookThatApp advertise rental-oriented or multi-day capabilities. Tipo also lists full-day and multi-day booking on Basic. These are candidates to test against your inventory model, not interchangeable rental-management systems.

| Situation | Candidates to investigate | Important test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rentals alongside appointments and classes | Cowlendar, BookX | One resource shared between service types |
| Equipment with deposits or bonds | BookThatApp, Cowlendar | Distinguish a booking payment from a damage bond |
| Seasonal rental prices | BookX | A date range crossing two price periods |
| A small catalogue and staff team | Tipo | Resource count and overlapping intervals |

The [comparison library](/compare) links to each vendor's official listing and shows plan limits. Use current quotes for any special inventory or payment requirement.

## Test overlapping dates, not only identical start times
Create a reservation from Monday morning to Wednesday evening. A second reservation starting Tuesday must not be accepted for the same unit just because its start time differs. Then try a reservation after the configured turnaround buffer; it should become available when the equipment is genuinely ready.

Test cancellations and early returns too. The inventory should recover according to your intended process. If an app requires a manual action to reopen stock, make that task explicit for the team.

## Deposits need a clear definition
An advance payment contributes to the rental price. A refundable damage deposit protects against a different risk. A card authorization is different again: it has processor-specific rules and is not the same as taking a payment and refunding it later.

Ask the app and payment provider to demonstrate the exact mechanism you need. Show the customer what is charged now, what remains due, when a refundable amount is returned, and how a dispute is handled. Our [rental deposit workflow](/blog/how-to-build-a-rental-damage-deposit-workflow-that-protects-you) separates these steps.

## Make pickup and return usable
Tell customers where to collect the item, what to bring and how late returns affect their booking. Include opening hours and the correct timezone. If the return location differs from pickup, collect that choice before payment and check that your operations can support it.

Use custom questions for useful logistics, not as a substitute for inventory rules. Asking “which bike do you want?” does not reserve that bike unless the answer is connected to an actual resource allocation.

## Compare the total cost
Model a quiet month and a busy month. Count bookings, revenue, team accounts, locations and integrations. Cowlendar's published tiers scale with booking revenue; BookThatApp lists booking-volume limits; BookX and Tipo use their own feature and team/service tiers. The cheapest entry plan may not cover peak season.

Before switching, import or manually reconcile every future reservation and blackout period. Keep a dated inventory snapshot, then test one real item from booking to return. Avoid running two independently bookable calendars against the same stock.
