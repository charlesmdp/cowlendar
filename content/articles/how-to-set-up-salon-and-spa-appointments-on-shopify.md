## Make the service menu easy to book
Use recognizable treatment names and explain duration, price and what is included. Separate a consultation from a treatment when an assessment is required. If the final price depends on the service performed, make that clear before the customer reserves.

Avoid a long list of nearly identical services. Group related treatments on the storefront, but preserve different durations and resource requirements in the booking configuration.

## Assign the right people and spaces
A stylist may offer haircuts but not every specialist treatment. A massage may require both a therapist and a particular room. Document these dependencies before enabling availability.

| Service | Staff requirement | Other availability to protect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Haircut | Qualified stylist | Chair and cleanup time |
| Color consultation | Appropriate specialist | Consultation space |
| Massage | Qualified therapist | Treatment room and turnaround |
| Nail appointment | Nail technician | Station and equipment preparation |

Check whether the app can reserve all required resources together. An “any staff member” option should only include people who can perform the selected service.

## Configure realistic timing
Set the customer-facing duration, preparation and cleanup buffers, and the minimum booking notice. Add breaks and planned absences. Verify that a long treatment cannot start too close to closing time.

If a treatment includes processing time during which staff may perform other work, ask whether the software can model that accurately. Do not shorten the reservation simply to create more apparent availability.

## Decide how payment and changes work
Full prepayment, a booking advance and payment after the service are different workflows. Choose the one that fits your policy and supported payment setup. Explain the cancellation cutoff and what happens to money already paid.

Test a canceled booking and a refund separately. For a reschedule, check that the correct therapist, room and duration remain attached to the new appointment.

## Write useful reminders
Include the service, time, location, arrival guidance and a clear change link. If a treatment has preparation instructions, send them early enough to be useful. Keep private intake information out of shared calendars and broadly visible messages.

For sensitive health-related information, use an appropriate system and access controls. A generic booking questionnaire should not automatically become a medical record.

## Launch with one representative service
Cowlendar provides booking questions, reminders, staff/resource availability and payment options. Apntly, Appointo and other apps may also fit your salon. Start with the service that has the most important resource constraints, then expand only after the end-to-end test passes.

Have the receptionist and a service provider both use the setup. They should be able to identify tomorrow's appointments, understand payment status and make a permitted change without maintaining a second contradictory calendar.
